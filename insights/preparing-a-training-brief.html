How to prepare a corporate training brief

A practical framework for describing your team, objectives and delivery requirements before requesting a proposal.

Begin with the work that needs to improveDefine the audienceSeparate requirements from preferencesAgree what a useful result looks likeUse the brief to support a conversation

Begin with the work that needs to improve

A useful training brief starts with a task, decision or capability need. “AI training” describes a subject; “help our team evaluate AI-assisted research before using it in a client report” describes a learning requirement. The more concrete starting point makes it easier to discuss appropriate content.

  • Describe the work the learning should support.
  • Explain what people find difficult or inconsistent today.
  • Identify the change you would like participants to make.

Define the audience

Different roles may need different examples and levels of depth. Include the approximate team size, participants’ responsibilities and their current experience. Distinguish people who need an introduction from those who already use the tools and want a more structured approach.

Separate requirements from preferences

Some conditions are essential, such as approved software, information security requirements or accessibility needs. Others are preferences, such as a particular session format. Making the distinction helps a provider propose an approach that fits the actual constraints.

  • Essential tools, policies or technical requirements
  • Timing and participant availability
  • Preferred delivery format and location
  • Any existing learning materials or internal expertise

Agree what a useful result looks like

Choose an outcome that can be observed or discussed after the learning. This might be a completed activity, a clearer workflow or a consistent way of reviewing information. Attendance alone does not explain whether participants can apply the material.

Use the brief to support a conversation

The first brief does not need to be a complete curriculum. Its purpose is to make the learning need understandable. Use the subsequent discussion to confirm suitability, scope, inclusions, responsibilities and commercial terms.
