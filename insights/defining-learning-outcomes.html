Defining useful learning outcomes

Describe what participants should be able to do, then connect the content and activities to that purpose.

Describe an observable capabilityKeep the scope realisticConnect each outcome with an activityUse assessment to clarify the expectationReview the whole learning pathway

Describe an observable capability

A learning outcome is more useful when it describes what someone can do. “Understand data” is broad. “Choose an appropriate chart for a stated business question and explain its limitations” gives the learning designer and participant a clearer target.

Keep the scope realistic

Consider the audience’s starting point, the available learning time and the complexity of the task. A short introductory session cannot reasonably cover every aspect of a broad professional capability. Prioritise the outcomes that matter for the immediate purpose.

Connect each outcome with an activity

Once the outcome is clear, ask what practice would help someone develop it. Reading an explanation, discussing a scenario and completing a practical task serve different functions. Choose activities because of their contribution to the outcome.

  • What should participants be able to do?
  • What information or instruction will support that capability?
  • What practice is needed?
  • What evidence would show useful progress?

Use assessment to clarify the expectation

An activity or knowledge check should align with what has been taught and practised. Clear response criteria make feedback more useful. They also help distinguish a misunderstanding of the content from an unclear instruction or unsuitable task.

Review the whole learning pathway

Check for gaps, unnecessary repetition and abrupt jumps in difficulty. A coherent program gives each module a purpose and connects it with what came before. Revisit the outcomes when the audience or delivery context changes.
